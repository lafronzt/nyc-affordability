Getting a mortgage approved is a separate hurdle from getting a co-op board's approval — a lender can sign off on your financing and a board can still reject you, without explanation, for reasons that have nothing to do with your ability to pay. The board package is the dossier that makes that second review possible: typically 2-3 years of tax returns, bank and brokerage statements, employment and personal reference letters, a financial statement, and a personal essay explaining why you want to live in the building.

## What boards actually check

Boards are looking for the same two things a lender checks — debt-to-income ratio and post-closing liquidity — but often apply stricter thresholds than a bank would.
