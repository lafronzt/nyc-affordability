Take-home pay — also called net pay — is what's actually deposited into your account each pay period, after every mandatory and voluntary deduction comes out of your gross salary. It's the number that matters for budgeting rent, a mortgage, or a savings goal; gross salary is only the starting point.

**Take-home pay = Gross salary − federal tax − state tax − local tax (NYC residents) − FICA − pre-tax deductions (401(k), HSA, health insurance, etc.)**

Two deductions behave differently from the others in a way that trips people up:

- A **Traditional 401(k)** contribution lowers your income-tax bill (it's excluded from taxable income) but is still subject to FICA — it doesn't reduce Social Security or Medicare withholding.
- An **HSA** contribution is exempt from *both* income tax and FICA — the one deduction that reduces every payroll tax at once.
- A **Roth 401(k)** contribution reduces neither — it comes out of already-taxed pay, which is the whole point of a Roth.

## Why the gap is bigger in NYC

Because NYC residents stack federal, NY State, *and* NYC local tax on top of FICA, take-home pay here typically runs lower than the same gross salary would net in a state with no city-level income tax — often landing around 65-75% of gross once all four layers are combined, depending on income and filing status.
